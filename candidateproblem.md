Candidate Problem




Problem Background

Sometimes people cook more food than can be eaten in one sitting. Someone may cook a lot of food early in the day, or purposely cook more food than needed or even have leftover food cooked for themselves. They may wish to make money out of the food. People around like workers or students may wish to buy meals prepared locally, with ingredients that they enjoy. The proposed problem is whether there could be some means through which such people and those who want the meals could get connected.

The household will want to provide a certain amount of food portions which they know will spoil soon; the purchaser will need to know how many food portions there are, when they were cooked, what is in them, and when it will be ready for collection or delivery. Currently, there might be a situation where households store, freeze, share, donate, or dispose of their leftovers, while purchasers themselves cook, purchase prepared foods, eat at restaurants, or order from local cooks. The problem will be particularly acute when the food will be available for only a short period of time or there will be two purchasers who want the last portion of the food. The extra un-served food portion is distinct from the food that has been already served to someone's plate: this study doesn't make assumptions that all the leftovers will be good enough for sale.

Evidence and status of claims

Report: The Food Policy Office of New York City states high amounts of food waste within households, yet the statistics provided do not indicate the quantity of safe, saleable cooked food [10]. USDA study focuses on the time spent by adults in obtaining and preparation of food [1]. Documented: Shef already facilitates connections between the local cooks and buyers [3]. Reported: The fact that Shef's cooks prepare menus and receive advance orders is stated by Shef itself [3]. Documented: New York State's Home Processor Exemption includes certain food products like baked goods and snacks but excludes food ready to be sold after being refrigerated [9]. It might mean that most cooked foods cannot use that exemption. Hypothesis: Residents of one neighborhood wish to generate extra money from selling their additional, untouched food, while the buyers will buy this food. The claims were not measured locally. I did not see any sales, examine any meal or check their nutritional value.

Existing Solutions

These two services below resolve many aspects of the issue. The contrast between them raises concerns regarding which requirements have been fulfilled in a selected area, and not evidence of their inadequacy.

However, Shef and CookUnity have partially solved the mentioned problem differently. Shef provides the opportunity for customers to get in touch with local cooks, offering their home-made meals. Moreover, customers make pre-orders. Thus, the service helps customers to have access to home-cooked food and cooks to know what kind of food to cook. I should find out what kinds of meals, pricing options, diet-related information, and delivery dates are available in the certain ZIP code, as there are certain local food regulations as well [3]. The company CookUnity provides pre-cooked meals chosen by the clients for weekly delivery and heating. The company delivers menu options in advance, nutritional value, and an ability to pass on the meal of the week. I would have to research whether it is feasible for clients to go for a weekly meal plan or whether they would like occasional meals or meals prepared by local cooks in particular.
